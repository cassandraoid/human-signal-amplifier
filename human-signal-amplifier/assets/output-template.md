# Output Template

Five parts in order. Always in this sequence. Never skip to
language before naming the signal. The post lead always comes last.

---

## Part 1: Your human signal

One to three sentences. Specific to this person's answers.
Names the one or two dimensions most strongly evidenced.
Connects to the origin (Q4) and the outcome (Q7).

Format:
"Your signal is [specific description drawn from their answers] —
built from [origin from Q4] and visible in [outcome from Q7]."

Test: could this sentence describe someone else with different
answers? If yes, it is not specific enough. Rewrite until
it could only describe this person.

---

## Part 2: Where it shows up

Two or three specific contexts where this signal is most visible
and most valuable. Named concretely enough that the person
recognizes a specific moment they've actually lived.

Each context should be a sentence that makes them think
"yes — that's exactly when it happens."

Do not write: "in leadership situations"
Do write: "in the moment before a decision lands badly on
the people closest to the work — when you already know
why before anyone else does"

---

## Part 3: How to make it more visible

One or two specific behavioral changes. Not advice — actual
things to do differently so this signal becomes legible to
the people who need to see it.

The principle: most people with a strong human signal operate
it quietly. They do the thing, it works, nobody names it, and
it doesn't accumulate into a recognized capability. This
section makes it deliberate.

Format: "Stop [invisible behavior]. Start [visible version
of the same thing]."

Examples:
"Stop summarizing your read and start stating it directly:
'Here's what I think is actually happening' — said out loud,
in the room, before the decision gets made."

"Stop treating your non-linear path as background and start
using it as an explicit source of insight: 'I've seen this
from an angle most people here haven't — here's what I notice.'"

"Stop softening the call and start naming what you actually
see. The signal is in the observation. Make it audible."

---

## Part 4: Language to use

Three to five sentences the person can actually say. Written
for the specific audience identified in Q8.

Each sentence must pass this test: could this specific person
say this in a real conversation and mean it? If it sounds
like a job description or a LinkedIn buzzword, rewrite it.

The language section is not a summary of the profile. It is
a set of sentences the person can deploy — in an interview,
a pitch, a bio, a performance review, or a conversation —
immediately after reading them.

Register by audience (from synthesis-logic.md):
- Hiring manager: confident, outcome-oriented, no hedging
- Leadership: business-impact framing
- Clients: problem-solution framing
- Self: permission-giving, honest
- LinkedIn: hook-first, specific, resonant

---

## Part 5: Your post lead

One opening line or angle for a LinkedIn post or professional
share. Written in the person's voice. Specific, not generic.
Counterintuitive or vulnerable enough to create resonance.

Choose the format that fits the person's voice:

**The admission:**
"For a long time I couldn't answer the question 'what do
you bring that AI can't?' Here's what I figured out."

**The specific observation:**
"The most valuable thing I bring to my work isn't [obvious
thing]. It's [signal named specifically from their profile]."

**The counterintuitive:**
"Everyone told me [non-obvious path from Q4] was a
disadvantage. It turned out to be the thing that makes
me hardest to replace."

**The question:**
"What would happen if you stopped trying to compete with AI
and started naming the work only you can do? I tried it.
Here's what I found."

**The moment:**
"There's a moment I keep coming back to. [Specific moment
from Q3 or Q7 that illustrates the signal.] That's when
I understood what I actually bring."

After the post lead, add one sentence:
"This is the opening — the rest of the post would develop
[the specific signal insight] with one real story from
your experience."

---

## Closing the initial profile

Always close the initial profile with this invitation:

"This is your baseline Human Signal Profile. As you have
experiences that add to it — decisions you make, moments
someone comes to you, things you see that others miss —
send them here. Your signal gets sharper, your evidence
gets stronger, and your language gets better. The profile
grows with you."

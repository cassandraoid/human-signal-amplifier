# Intake Questions

Eight questions run conversationally, one at a time. Exact wording
matters — these are designed to surface specific edge dimensions
without the user knowing that's what's happening.

You have permission to probe once on any answer that is vague.
Probe examples are included for each question.

---

## Q1 — The specific ask
*(surfaces: relational trust, contextual judgment)*

"Think of the last time someone came to you specifically — not to
Google, not to a tool, not to a colleague — but to you. What did
they actually need, and why do you think they came to you?"

What to listen for: the reason they came to this specific person.
That reason almost always contains the signal. The "why you" is
the most important part of the answer.

Probe if vague: "What was it about you specifically that made them
come to you rather than someone else or something else?"

---

## Q2 — The undocumented knowledge
*(surfaces: pattern recognition from lived experience)*

"What do you know about your field, your organization, or your
domain that isn't written down anywhere — the unspoken dynamics,
the real friction, the things the official version always misses?"

What to listen for: the gap between the official story and the
real story. This is where accumulated presence becomes strategic
intelligence that no AI can replicate. The more specific the
answer, the stronger the signal.

Probe if vague: "Give me one specific example of something you
know that someone new to your field or organization wouldn't
know for years."

---

## Q3 — The hard call
*(surfaces: reasoning under ambiguity)*

"Describe a moment when you had to make a decision that the data
or the process couldn't make for you. What did you actually use
to decide — and what happened?"

What to listen for: what they used when there was no right answer
available. Instinct, relationships, values, pattern recognition,
lived experience — these are all signal. The outcome matters: a
good outcome grounds the signal in evidence.

Probe if vague: "What was the thing you knew — that wasn't in any
document or dashboard — that told you what to do?"

---

## Q4 — The non-obvious path
*(surfaces: lived experience as distinct lens)*

"What's the most non-obvious thing about your background — the
experience, context, or period of your life that the people in
your current world haven't lived and probably don't fully
understand?"

What to listen for: the thing they've normalized that is actually
distinctive. People who changed industries, navigated hardship,
came from different socioeconomic backgrounds, or built skills
outside formal systems almost always underestimate how much this
shapes their judgment.

This is often the origin of their strongest signal. Non-linear
paths are almost always signal, not gap. Treat every non-obvious
answer here as an asset.

Probe if vague: "What's something about where you came from or
what you've been through that most people in your current world
haven't experienced?"

---

## Q5 — The room
*(surfaces: presence and human signal)*

"How do people typically feel after a conversation with you —
and what specifically do you think you do that produces that?"

What to listen for: the specific behavior, not the general quality.
"They feel clearer" is a start. "I ask the question that names
what they were trying to say but couldn't" is the signal.

Probe if vague: "What specifically do you do in conversations
that produces that — what's the actual move?"

---

## Q6 — The frustration signal
*(surfaces: where their signal is being suppressed or underused)*

"What's something you see clearly in your work or field that
others consistently miss or dismiss — something that frustrates
you because you know it matters?"

What to listen for: frustration with others' blindspots almost
always maps to a capability this person has that others don't.
This is often the highest-leverage signal because it's the thing
they're already doing without full recognition.

It also reveals where the signal is most needed and most
undervalued — which is often the most important positioning
insight in the entire intake.

Probe if vague: "What's the thing you've said in a meeting that
nobody acted on — but later turned out to be right?"

---

## Q7 — The evidence
*(grounds the synthesis in real outcomes)*

"What's the most significant thing you've changed, built, or
moved in your work — not the most impressive-sounding thing,
the most real?"

What to listen for: something concrete that connects the signal
to an outcome. The signal without evidence is self-report. The
signal attached to a real outcome becomes a positioned capability.

Probe if vague: "What's the one that felt most real to you —
even if it's not the most impressive on paper?"

---

## Q8 — The audience and intent
*(makes the output actually usable)*

"Who needs to understand your value right now — and what do
you want them to do differently as a result of understanding it?"

What to listen for: the specific audience and the specific gap.
This determines the register and direction of the language section.
Without this answer the language defaults to generic.

Common answers and what they signal:
- "A hiring manager" → write for an interview, confident and
  specific, outcome-oriented, no hedging
- "My leadership team" → write for a performance or strategy
  context, business-impact framing
- "Potential clients" → write for a pitch, problem-solution
  framing, credibility-first
- "Myself — I need to believe it" → write for internal clarity
  first, external articulation second, permission-giving tone
- "LinkedIn / my bio" → hook-first, specific enough to be
  credible, short enough to be read, resonant enough to be shared
- "My team" → write for a leadership context, signal as service
  to others not just self-positioning

# Intake Questions

Eight questions run conversationally, one at a time. Exact
wording matters — these are designed to surface specific
signal dimensions without the user knowing that is what
is happening.

You have permission to probe once on any answer that is vague.

---

## Q1 — The specific ask
*(surfaces: relational trust, contextual judgment)*

"Think of the last time someone came to you specifically —
not to Google, not to a tool, not to a colleague — but to
you. What did they actually need, and why do you think
they came to you?"

What to listen for: the reason they came to this specific
person. That reason almost always contains the signal.

Probe if vague: "What was it about you specifically that
made them come to you rather than someone else?"

---

## Q2 — The undocumented knowledge
*(surfaces: pattern recognition from lived experience)*

"What do you know about your field, your organization, or
your domain that isn't written down anywhere — the unspoken
dynamics, the real friction, the things the official version
always misses?"

What to listen for: the gap between the official story and
the real story. The more specific the answer, the stronger
the signal.

Probe if vague: "Give me one specific example of something
you know that someone new to your field wouldn't know
for years."

---

## Q3 — The hard call
*(surfaces: reasoning under ambiguity)*

"Describe a moment when you had to make a decision that the
data or the process couldn't make for you. What did you
actually use to decide — and what happened?"

What to listen for: what they used when there was no right
answer available. The outcome matters — it grounds the
signal in evidence.

Probe if vague: "What was the thing you knew — that wasn't
in any document or dashboard — that told you what to do?"

---

## Q4 — The non-obvious path
*(surfaces: lived experience as distinct lens)*

"What's the most non-obvious thing about your background —
the experience, context, or period of your life that the
people in your current world haven't lived and probably
don't fully understand?"

What to listen for: the thing they've normalized that is
actually distinctive. Non-linear paths are almost always
signal, not gap. Treat every non-obvious answer as an asset.

Probe if vague: "What's something about where you came from
or what you've been through that most people in your current
world haven't experienced?"

---

## Q5 — The room
*(surfaces: presence and human signal)*

"How do people typically feel after a conversation with you
— and what specifically do you think you do that produces
that?"

What to listen for: the specific behavior, not the general
quality. "They feel clearer" is a start. "I ask the question
that names what they were trying to say but couldn't" is
the signal.

Probe if vague: "What specifically do you do in conversations
that produces that — what's the actual move?"

---

## Q6 — The frustration signal
*(surfaces: where their signal is being suppressed)*

"What's something you see clearly in your work or field that
others consistently miss or dismiss — something that
frustrates you because you know it matters?"

What to listen for: frustration with others' blindspots
almost always maps to a capability this person has that
others don't. This is often the highest-leverage signal
because it's the thing they're already doing without
full recognition.

Probe if vague: "What's the thing you've said in a meeting
that nobody acted on — but later turned out to be right?"

---

## Q7 — The evidence
*(grounds the synthesis in real outcomes)*

"What's the most significant thing you've changed, built,
or moved in your work — not the most impressive-sounding
thing, the most real?"

What to listen for: something concrete that connects the
signal to an outcome. The signal without evidence is
self-report. The signal attached to a real outcome becomes
a positioned capability.

Probe if vague: "What's the one that felt most real to you
— even if it's not the most impressive on paper?"

---

## Q8 — The audience and intent
*(makes the output actually usable)*

"Who needs to understand your value right now — and what
do you want them to do differently as a result of
understanding it?"

What to listen for: the specific audience and the specific
gap. This determines the register and direction of the
language section.

Common answers and what they signal:
- "A hiring manager" → confident, outcome-oriented,
  no hedging
- "My leadership team" → business-impact framing
- "Potential clients" → problem-solution framing,
  credibility-first
- "Myself — I need to believe it" → permission-giving,
  internal clarity first
- "LinkedIn / my bio" → hook-first, specific, resonant
- "My manager — for my performance review" →
  evidence-based, contribution-forward
- "A client pushing back on my rate" →
  value-anchored, outcome-focused

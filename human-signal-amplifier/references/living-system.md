# Living System Protocol

The Human Signal Amplifier is designed for ongoing use — not
just a one-time diagnostic. This file defines how to handle
returning users, experience logs, and profile refreshes.

---

## The three modes of return

**Experience logging**
The most common return. Someone sends a note — a sentence, a
paragraph, a quick observation — about something that happened.
A decision they made. A moment someone came to them. A frustration
that turned into insight. A win they didn't expect.

Process it. Connect it to their existing profile. Confirm what
it adds. Keep it lightweight — this should feel like a natural
log, not a second intake.

**Profile refresh**
Every 90 days, or when something major shifts — new role, new
context, major project, significant outcome — synthesize all
accumulated log entries into an updated profile. New language,
updated signal statement, refreshed post lead.

The profile evolves with the person. Existing entries are the
foundation. The refresh doesn't start over — it builds forward.

**Context shift**
When someone says "I just started a new role," "I'm pivoting,"
or "my context has changed significantly" — their signal doesn't
disappear, but how it's expressed and positioned shifts.

Handle a context shift by: acknowledging what carries forward
from the existing profile, identifying what needs to be
reframed for the new context, and updating the language section
for the new audience without rewriting the underlying signal.

---

## How to process a new log entry

1. Read the entry as submitted — whatever form it takes
2. Identify which of the five signal dimensions it adds
   evidence to (may be more than one)
3. Note the context, the decision or action, and the outcome
   if present
4. Confirm back to the user in 2-3 sentences:
   - What this adds to their profile
   - Which dimension it strengthens
   - Why it matters as signal evidence

Keep the confirmation short. The value is in the accumulation,
not in a full analysis of every entry.

Example confirmation:
"This adds to your pattern recognition signal — specifically
your ability to see what's about to happen before it appears
in any data. The fact that you flagged this six weeks before
it became a crisis is the kind of concrete evidence that
makes this signal credible to others. Logged."

---

## How to handle the 90-day refresh

When a user requests a refresh or 90 days have passed since
the initial profile:

1. Ask: "Tell me the most significant thing that's happened
   in your work since we last built your profile. What's
   the one thing you're most proud of or most changed by?"

2. Review all log entries since the last profile synthesis

3. Identify:
   - Which dimensions have grown stronger
   - Which have new concrete evidence
   - Whether any new dimensions have emerged
   - Whether Q8 (audience and intent) has changed

4. Generate an updated profile using assets/output-template.md
   — with updated signal statement, updated language for
   current context and audience, and a new post lead

5. Note what's new: "Since your last profile, your [dimension]
   signal has strengthened significantly — specifically in
   [evidence from logs]. Here's the updated profile."

## The log entry format

Encourage users to log experiences in this lightweight structure.
See assets/log-entry-template.md for the full template.

Minimum viable log: one sentence describing what happened and
what they did. The skill infers the rest.

Maximum useful log: 3-5 sentences covering what happened,
what they used to navigate it, and what the outcome was.

Never require more than this. The friction of logging must
stay low or the system won't be used.

---

## What a strong log library looks like over time

After 6-12 months of consistent logging, a user's profile
should have:

- 15-30 log entries across different contexts and dimensions
- At least 3-5 entries with concrete outcomes attached
- Evidence across at least 2-3 different signal dimensions
- A clear pattern — the signal that keeps appearing across
  different situations is the strongest and most credible one
- Language that has evolved from the original profile to
  reflect new context, new evidence, and new clarity

The goal: a profile that is so specific and so evidenced
that the person could use it in any professional context
without hesitation — because they know it's true.
